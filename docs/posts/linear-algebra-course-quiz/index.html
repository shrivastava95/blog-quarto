1 Scope

This guide follows the public lessons retrieved from the Linear Algebra playlist. Members-only and unavailable lessons are excluded.

2 Unit 1 — Foundations of vector spaces

Videos: 1, 2, 4, 5.

Vectors in (R^n) are tuples of scalars. A line through a point (p) with direction (v) is

\[ L=\{\mathbf p+t\mathbf v:t\in\mathbb R\}. \]

A vector space is closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication and satisfies the vector-space axioms. A subspace (UV) must contain (0) and satisfy

\[ \mathbf u,\mathbf v\in U,\ a,b\in\mathbb R \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\mathbf u+b\mathbf v\in U. \]

Representative problem: determine whether the solution set of (x+2y-z=0) is a subspace of (R^3), and parameterize it.

3 Unit 2 — Subspaces, inner products, and geometry

Videos: 6, 9, 10.

The Euclidean inner product and norm are

\[ \langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v\rangle=\sum_i u_iv_i, \qquad \|\mathbf v\|=\sqrt{\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf v\rangle}. \]

The angle relation is

\[ \langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v\rangle=\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|\cos\theta. \]

In (R^3), (uv) is perpendicular to both inputs and has magnitude equal to the parallelogram area:

\[ \|\mathbf u\times\mathbf v\|=\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|\sin\theta. \]

Representative problem: compute the projection of (u) onto nonzero (v):

\[ \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf u)= \frac{\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v\rangle}{\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf v\rangle}\mathbf v. \]

4 Unit 3 — Matrices and linear maps

Videos: 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20.

A linear map satisfies

\[ T(a\mathbf u+b\mathbf v)=aT(\mathbf u)+bT(\mathbf v). \]

For a matrix (A=[a_1  a_n]),

\[ A\mathbf x=x_1\mathbf a_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf a_n. \]

Composition is matrix multiplication:

\[ (AB)\mathbf x=A(B\mathbf x). \]

The matrix of a linear map is determined by the images of the standard basis vectors. Special matrices include diagonal, triangular, symmetric, and identity matrices.

Representative problem: find the matrix of (T(x,y)=(2x-y,x+3y)), then compute (T(1,2)).

5 Unit 4 — Independence, bases, coordinates, and dimension

Videos: 22, 23, 25, 26, 28.

Vectors (v_1,,v_k) are linearly independent when

\[ c_1\mathbf v_1+\cdots+c_k\mathbf v_k=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_1=\cdots=c_k=0. \]

A basis is an independent spanning set. Coordinates relative to a basis (B=(b_1,,b_n)) satisfy

\[ \mathbf v=[\mathbf v]_B{}_1\mathbf b_1+\cdots+[\mathbf v]_B{}_n\mathbf b_n. \]

The exchange lemma explains why all bases of a finite-dimensional space have the same number of vectors: the dimension.

Representative problem: express ((5,1)) in the basis ({(1,1),(2,-1)}).

6 Unit 5 — Inverses and transposes

Videos: 29, 31, 32.

An inverse satisfies

\[ AA^{-1}=A^{-1}A=I. \]

For a (2) matrix,

\[ \begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}^{-1} =\frac1{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}, \qquad ad-bc\ne0. \]

The transpose is defined by

\[ (A^T)_{ij}=A_{ji}, \qquad (AB)^T=B^TA^T. \]

Representative problem: find the inverse of ( \[\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\]

) and verify it by multiplication.

7 Unit 6 — Range, kernel, rank-nullity, and solving systems

Videos: 34, 35, 37, 38, 40, 42.

The kernel and range are

\[ \ker(A)=\{\mathbf x:A\mathbf x=0\}, \qquad \operatorname{range}(A)=\{A\mathbf x:\mathbf x\in V\}. \]

The rank-nullity theorem is

\[ \dim(V)=\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{nullity}(A). \]

Row operations preserve the solution set of a system when applied to its augmented matrix. In row-echelon form, pivot variables are determined and free variables parameterize the remaining solutions.

Representative problem: row-reduce an augmented matrix and state whether the system has no solution, one solution, or infinitely many solutions.

8 Unit 7 — Determinants

Videos: 43, 44, 46, 47, 48, 50, 51.

For a (2) matrix,

\[ \det\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}=ad-bc. \]

The determinant is multilinear and alternating, and satisfies

\[ \det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B). \]

For an (nn) matrix, Laplace expansion along row (i) is

\[ \det(A)=\sum_j(-1)^{i+j}a_{ij}\det(A_{ij}). \]

Geometrically, (|(A)|) is volume scaling. A square matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is nonzero.

Representative problem: compute the determinant of ( \[\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\0&4&5\\1&0&6\end{bmatrix}\]

) by expansion or elimination.

9 Unit 8 — Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

Videos: 53, 54, 55.

An eigenvector is a nonzero vector satisfying

\[ A\mathbf v=\lambda\mathbf v. \]

Eigenvalues solve the characteristic equation

\[ p_A(\lambda)=\det(A-\lambda I)=0. \]

The algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue is its multiplicity as a root of (p_A). Eigenvectors for () form the eigenspace

\[ E_\lambda=\ker(A-\lambda I). \]

Representative problem: find the eigenvalues and eigenspaces of ( \[\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\]

).

10 Quiz

Attempt this closed-notes. Each question is worth 2 points.

  1. Parameterize the line through ((1,2)) parallel to ((3,-1)).
  2. Prove that the solution set of a homogeneous linear system is a subspace.
  3. Compute ((1,2,-1),(2,0,3)) and both vector norms.
  4. Compute ((1,2,0)(2,-1,1)).
  5. Find the matrix of (T(x,y)=(2x-y,x+3y)).
  6. Explain why the columns of a matrix determine its matrix-vector product.
  7. Test whether ((1,2)) and ((2,4)) are linearly independent.
  8. Find the coordinates of ((5,1)) in the basis ({(1,1),(2,-1)}).
  9. Find the inverse of ( \[\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\] ).
  10. State and interpret the rank-nullity theorem.
  11. Describe how free variables appear in a row-echelon solution.
  12. Compute ( \[\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\] ).
  13. State the determinant criterion for invertibility.
  14. Find the characteristic polynomial of ( \[\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\] ).
  15. Find the eigenspaces of that matrix.
  16. Explain why eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent.
